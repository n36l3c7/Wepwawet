Wepwawet

Measurement-based IP geolocation for Linux: passive evidence plus ping and traceroute from many vantage points, on a map. Named after the Egyptian god Wepwawet, the jackal "opener of the ways".

v0.3.0: measurements. After the passive sources, Wepwawet pings the address from about 80 probes around the world, intersects the speed-of-light limits into a feasible region, names the cities inside it and detects anycast. Traceroutes arrive in v0.4.0.

What it does

Wepwawet estimates where an IP address is by combining two kinds of evidence:

  • Passive evidence: GeoIP databases and how much they disagree, RIR registration data (RDAP), routing data (RIPEstat), reverse DNS of the target and its neighbours, PeeringDB facilities.
  • Active measurements: ping and traceroute from many probes around the world. Round-trip times put hard physical limits on where the target can be: light in fibre covers about 100 km per millisecond of round trip.

Every estimate comes with an uncertainty radius, a confidence score and the evidence behind it, on the map. The tool is for network engineers, SOC analysts and researchers who need the network location of an address (city, PoP, facility), not the identity of who uses it.

Ethical use

  • Only public data sources and measurement platforms, used within their terms of service.
  • No port scanning and no interaction with the target beyond ping, traceroute and DNS lookups.
  • For a home connection the realistic limit is the provider's point of presence. The subscriber's address is only in the provider's records, which Wepwawet does not try to obtain. Do not use it to locate or follow people.

Known limits

  • Residential: the realistic ceiling is the city of the provider's PoP or BNG.
  • Mobile and CGNAT: the gateway can be hundreds of kilometres from the user.
  • VPN, Tor, proxies, hosting: the result is the exit server.
  • Anycast: there is no single location; Wepwawet flags it instead of guessing.
  • MPLS tunnels, ICMP filtering and asymmetric routing reduce precision.
